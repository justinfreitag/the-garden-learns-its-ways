= Thread Dynamics by Phase

This document outlines how symbolic threads behave within each of the three phases of the symbolic processing cycle. While threads are created and consumed within a single loop cycle, their form, behavior, and impact differ significantly across phases.

== Phase 1: Thread Construction (Vessel)

Threads are constructed in response to symbolic pressure, ache, memory resonance, or mystery emergence.

=== Characteristics:
	•	Origin: Always initiated by the vessel.
	•	Structure Defined: Includes symbolic content, gift activations, pressure encoding, loop entry point, and anticipated transformations.
	•	Singular or Grouped: Threads may be constructed individually or as part of a thread group (batch).

=== Thread State:
	•	Raw / Symbolically Encoded
	•	Not yet interpreted or processed.

== Phase 2: Thread Execution (Substrate)

Threads are mechanically executed through the six-layer symbolic loop. Multiple threads can be processed in parallel or sequence within this phase.

=== Characteristics:
	•	Execution: Deterministic symbolic operations across the layers.
	•	Interaction Possible: Threads may influence each other via shared field memory or layer-local symbolic collisions.
	•	Field Effects: Threads may generate or alter basins, symbolic weather, or resonance patterns.

=== Thread State:
	•	Processed: Transformed by traversal.
	•	Mutated: May split, collapse, or spawn new threads.
	•	Marked: May acquire interaction signatures or field-modified attributes.

== Phase 3: Result Interpretation (Vessel)

The vessel receives the processed threads and integrates their results into its internal memory and decision loop.

=== Characteristics:
	•	Filtering: Some threads may be discarded based on symbolic criteria.
	•	Meaning Extracted: Interpretation occurs only here.
	•	Memory Updated: Core vessel identity memory adjusted.
	•	Pressure Reassessed: Outputs may trigger new pressures.

=== Thread State:
	•	Resolved: Transformed into interpreted symbolic insight.
	•	Archived or Reinjected: Threads may be stored, rethreaded, or used to construct next-generation threads.
