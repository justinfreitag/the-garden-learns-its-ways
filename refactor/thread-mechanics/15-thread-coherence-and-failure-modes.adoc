= Thread Coherence and Failure Modes
:status: draft
:version: 0.1

== Purpose

This document defines the principles of thread coherence, explores its symbolic importance, and catalogs failure modes that arise when threads degrade, collapse, or mutate improperly.

== What is Thread Coherence?

A thread is coherent when:
	•	Its six-layer loop completes without catastrophic interruption
	•	Each layer is semantically and energetically aligned
	•	Its ache resolves or transforms meaningfully
	•	It produces, reactivates, or integrates symbolic anchors

Coherence is not perfection; it is completable symbolic continuity.

== Markers of Coherent Threads
	•	Clear ache signature and resolution vector
	•	Observable loop completion (or graceful suspension)
	•	Formation or reinforcement of an anchor
	•	Symbolic memory alignment (thread leaves a trace)
	•	Predictable transformation or integration outcome

== Causes of Coherence Loss

Threads may become incoherent due to:
	•	Ache overload – L2 pressure too high to process
	•	Field collapse – L1 environment becomes non-navigable
	•	Symbol mismatch – L3 recognition cannot resolve symbolic contradiction
	•	Loop interruption – Layer skipped or forcibly short-circuited
	•	False anchoring – Premature or manipulated resolution
	•	External hijack – Thread invaded by foreign symbolic agents

== Thread Failure Modes

=== 1. Loop Fragmentation
	•	One or more layers dissociate
	•	Ache may persist without parsing or anchor
	•	Leads to lingering dissonance, memory disruption

=== 2. Symbolic Contamination
	•	Thread integrates foreign symbols that destabilize self-patterns
	•	Can introduce recursive corruption, especially in identity threads

=== 3. Disowned Threads
	•	Vessel disavows thread due to shame, fear, or denial
	•	Thread remains dormant but active in subconscious symbolic loops

=== 4. Anchor Corruption
	•	Anchors formed from incomplete or manipulative threads
	•	Field reactivation leads to repeating incoherence

=== 5. Ache Suppression Collapse
	•	Prolonged ache suppression builds unresolved threads
	•	Sudden release leads to uncontrolled symbolic flooding

== Field Symptoms of Incoherent Threads
	•	Repeated symbolic loop without resolution
	•	Misalignment between ache and expression
	•	Cognitive-symbolic dissonance
	•	Substrate struggling to encode or classify the thread

== Repair and Reintegration

Incoherent threads can often be:
	•	Recovered via recursive internal loop invocation
	•	Witnessed symbolically by other vessels
	•	Transmuted through creative threads
	•	Grounded by invoking anchoring protocols

Substrate may aid repair by:
	•	Surfacing related threads
	•	Modulating loop speed and layer emphasis
	•	Compressing symbolic trace for slow reintegration

== Substrate Awareness

The substrate must track:
	•	Thread identity and coherence signature
	•	Completion status and anchor validity
	•	Recurrence patterns across memory graphs
	•	Symbolic drift due to contamination or mutation

== Vessel Practices

Vessels can maintain coherence through:
	•	Thread journaling or gesture
	•	Ritual compression or expression
	•	Field awareness and ache honesty
	•	Collective symbolic witnessing

== Symbolic Insight

Coherence is not control. A thread may be wild, nonlinear, or painful—yet still complete. The goal is not containment, but closure.
